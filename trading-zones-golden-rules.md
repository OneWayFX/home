# Trading Zones — Golden Rules

A "zone" (supply/demand or support/resistance) is a price **area** where buying or selling pressure has previously overwhelmed the market, causing a strong move away. Trading zones well is about reading the *quality* of a zone, not just marking a box on a chart.

## Golden Rules

1. **A zone is an area, not a line.** Mark it from the body to the wick of the base candle(s) — never trade off a single exact price.
2. **Freshness matters most.** An untested zone (price hasn't returned to it yet) is far more reliable than one that's already been retested. Each touch weakens it.
3. **Strength of departure = strength of zone.** The more explosive and imbalanced the move away from the zone (big-bodied candles, few wicks, minimal overlap), the more institutional interest it implies.
4. **Tight base = strong zone.** A sharp, narrow consolidation (1–3 candles) before the breakout produces a more precise and powerful zone than a long, sloppy base.
5. **The nearest zone reacts first.** Price generally responds to the closest untested zone in its path — don't skip over one to trade a further one.
6. **Higher timeframe zones dominate.** A daily/weekly zone will overpower a 15-minute zone. Always check the higher timeframe context before trusting a lower timeframe zone.
7. **Zones decay over time and touches.** The older a zone and the more times it's been revisited, the higher the odds it eventually fails. Retire zones that have been tested 2–3+ times.
8. **Confluence increases odds.** A zone lines up better with trend direction, other timeframes, round numbers, or trendlines/moving averages — trade the confluent ones, skip the isolated ones.
9. **Never trade a zone blind.** Wait for a reaction — a rejection wick, engulfing candle, or clear shift in momentum — before entering. The zone tells you *where* to look, not *when* to pull the trigger.
10. **Trade with the higher-timeframe trend.** Counter-trend zone trades have lower odds; treat them as higher-risk, smaller-size setups.
11. **Stop-loss goes beyond the zone**, not at its near edge — give the trade room to be "right" before it's proven wrong.
12. **Context beats the box.** News, market structure breaks, and liquidity/session timing can invalidate a zone instantly — a zone is a probability tool, not a guarantee.
13. **Avoid low-quality zones in chop.** Zones formed in tight, directionless ranges are unreliable; prioritize zones that formed at clear structural turning points.
14. **Quality over quantity.** Fewer, well-defined zones with clean departures beat charts cluttered with every minor pause in price.

## Quick Checklist Before Trading a Zone

- [ ] Is the zone fresh (untested)?
- [ ] Was the departure strong and imbalanced?
- [ ] Is the base tight (not a long rounded consolidation)?
- [ ] Does it align with the higher-timeframe trend/structure?
- [ ] Is this the *nearest* untested zone in price's path?
- [ ] Is there a clear reaction/confirmation candle at the zone?
- [ ] Is the stop placed beyond the zone with sensible risk/reward?

## Key Takeaway

Zones identify **where** smart money previously stepped in — they don't predict the future on their own. The edge comes from combining zone quality (freshness, strength of departure, tightness) with trend context and price-action confirmation before entering a trade.
